Sirius 4-GPU Scaling Diagnosis

Going from 1 to 4 GPUs gives 1.79×, not 4×. Each bottleneck below has a measured price in seconds of 4-GPU wall time, and two adversarial reviewers re-derived the top eight. The biggest item is hardware: four GPUs share two PCIe uplinks. Most of the rest is cross-GPU exchange work and a driver allocator lock that only bite when more than one GPU is active.

Ranked bottlenecks

What blocks linear scaling, largest first

1

Two PCIe uplinks feed four GPUs

hardware

GPU0+1 sit behind one PCIe switch and GPU2+3 behind another, each with a single Gen5 x16 uplink. Pinned host→GPU bandwidth: 52 GB/s for one GPU, 52 for GPUs 0+1, 102 for 0+2, 105 for all four. Two GPUs on one switch scale 1.22×; across switches 1.61×. With the host link removed (GPU-tier pin), 19 clean queries lose 3.8 s. Q17, Q19 and Q20 sit at the link floor at both GPU counts.

Lever: send fewer bytes (compression coverage, below). The hardware caps this box at about 12.2–12.4 s (≈2.9×) at today's wire bytes.

2.9 ssynth 3.48
2

Process-wide CUDA driver allocator lock

mechanical

Every device allocation goes from the cucascade reservation adaptor straight to cudaMallocFromPoolAsync/cudaFreeAsync with no caching layer. The driver serializes these across all GPUs at about 280–385k calls/s total. Mean call latency is 11 µs with 4 threads on 1 GPU and 56 µs with 4 threads on each of 4 GPUs; pool peer-access grants make no difference. In nsys, alloc+free thread-time grows from 10 s to 78 s, 81% of it waiting on driver locks. The allocator is busy 53% of 4-GPU wall time. Raising threads to 8 per GPU makes 4 GPUs 1.19 s slower.

Fix: a per-GPU user-space sub-allocator (arena or thread-local bins for small sizes) between the reservation adaptor and cuda_async; stop deep-copying P×C partition slices (gpu_partition_impl.cpp:212-221).

2.0 srange 1.5–2.4
3

Cross-GPU clones of hundreds of small fragments

design

627 scan tasks each emit P partition fragments, and the consuming task clones every remote fragment one at a time during its prepare step, with syncs per buffer. Clone thread-time is 0.23 s at 1 GPU and 25.4 s at 4. The Q1 "straggler GPU" is one merge task cloning 470 partials serially (148 ms). Q18's merge prepare grows from 27 ms to 6.1 s of thread-time. Finer partitions (256 MB) take the suite to 23.66 s.

Fix: coalesce fragments per producer GPU and partition before cloning; batch clones and order them with events instead of host syncs. Larger scan batches (4 GB) already cut this exchange cost by about 2.1 s.

1.2 ssynth 1.72
4

Cross-switch shuffles go through host memory

hardwaredesign

The platform reports P2P only within a switch (0↔1, 2↔3). Of 176.6 GB moved per suite, 117.6 GB (66.6%) is cross-switch and host-staged: device→host, sync, host→device, sync, per 1 MiB chunk. A staged clone costs 3.6× a peer clone per byte, with 6.4 host syncs each. Switch-aware partition placement does not help uniform hash shuffles; only key co-location would.

Fix: overlap the two copy legs with chunked double-buffering; co-locate lineitem and orders by key range.

0.9 s≈0.5 hw + 0.4 staging
5

Scans wait for the previous exchange to finish

design

The next scan pipeline is created only after the previous exchange barrier, and multi-GPU prefetch is off, so the uplinks sit idle during exchanges. Per-switch idle time barely shrinks: 10.25 s at 1 GPU, 8.05 s at 4 GPUs, including 4.6 s of interior gaps of 5 ms or more. The per-task prepare itself does not starve the link (5–7 prepares in flight per switch).

Fix: cross-pipeline scan lookahead so the next scan's uploads run during the current exchange.

0.7 ssynth 1.20
6

Exchange policy forces shuffles

designconfig

At 4 GPUs, every exchange above 64 MB must split into at least 4 partitions, and BUILD_PROBE joins use min(natural, N) partitions. Exchanges that pass data straight through at 1 GPU hash-partition at 4: PARTITION thread-time for that class is 0.13 s vs 16.0 s. Low caps (max_broadcast_join_size 256 MB, max_build_hash_table_bytes 2 GB) pick shuffles where a broadcast would do. Raising both to 8 GB saves 0.97 s at 4 GPUs (per-query best 1.4 s) but regresses Q7 and Q14.

Fix: cost-based broadcast choice; skip the P≥N floor when a local or broadcast plan exists.

0.7 ssynth 0.76
7

Fixed per-query work outside execution

design

Parse, bind, optimize and result return take about 442 ms per suite at both GPU counts, so they don't shrink with more GPUs.

0.34 sverified
8

Granularity tails

config

Partition counts not divisible by 4 put extra partitions on GPU0/GPU1 (the same switch); scan-task quantization and single-GPU tails. Small queries run at 50–85% occupancy.

0.24 ssynth 0.31
9

Bloom filter publication always host-staged

design

The transport is all-or-nothing (sirius_dynamic_bloom_filter.cu:1080): one GPU pair without pool access stages the whole publication. All 60 publications staged at 4 GPUs (mean 20 ms after the final contribution) vs peer copies on GPUs {0,1} (5.8 ms). Most of the latency is off the critical path.

Fix: reduce within each P2P island over peer copies; stage only the inter-island leg.

≤0.3 smeasured ≥0.05
10

One small pinned-memory pool for all GPUs

design

With one NUMA node, all GPUs share one small pinned pool. A reused slab carries another GPU's ready event, so device_scalar syncs wait on that GPU's queued work: 105k such syncs, 13.1 thread-s at 4 GPUs, none at 1.

Fix: key small pinned pools by CUDA device (sirius_context.cpp:1060-1073).

What to do

Fixes, by expected gain at 4 GPUs

  1. Config today: set scan_task_batch_size: 4GB, keep 4 pipeline threads per GPU, and choose 2-GPU subsets across switches. −3.1 s measured
  2. Sub-allocator between the reservation adaptor and cuda_async, plus no P×C deep-copy slices in partitioning. ≈2.3–3.7 s (also 0.9–2.1 s at 1 GPU)
  3. Exchange redesign: coalesce fragments before cloning, async and batched clone legs, cost-based broadcast, no forced P≥N floor when a local plan exists. ≈2–3 s
  4. Scan lookahead across pipelines so uploads continue during exchanges. ≈0.7–1 s
  5. Fewer wire bytes: compression plans for customer, part, partsupp and supplier; better encodings for o_comment and l_shipmode (−233 GB, 19% of scan bytes). ≈1.1 s
  6. Smaller items: hierarchical Bloom publication; per-device small pinned pools. ≈0.2–0.5 s

Even with every software fix, this machine tops out near 12.2–12.4 s at 4 GPUs (≈2.9×) at today's wire bytes. Reaching 4× needs fewer bytes on the uplinks or a PCIe topology with one uplink per GPU.

Method

How this was measured

  • Regime: one DuckDB session per run; all TPC-H columns pinned with pin_table(..., tier='host'); lineitem and orders Simpatico-compressed; 22 queries × 4 iterations, iteration 1 discarded as warm-up. Figure of merit: sum of hot-iteration means.
  • Runs: 1, 2 (same and cross switch) and 4 GPUs repeated twice; 12 config flips at 1 and 4 GPUs. 1-GPU totals reproduce within 0.04 s, 4-GPU within 0.10 s.
  • Profiling: nsys (CUDA, NVTX, OS runtime) on warm iteration 2 of every query at 1 and 4 GPUs, mapped to queries by absolute time against Quent labels. Quent telemetry from un-profiled runs for task lifecycles, per-GPU busy time and bytes. Microbenchmarks for host→GPU, P2P and driver allocator throughput.
  • Analysis: 7 query-family analysts and 3 cross-cutting analysts (allocator, exchange, link) priced every finding in 4-GPU wall seconds; a synthesis mapped 61 findings onto 11 root causes; two independent reviewers per top-8 item re-derived the numbers and corrected the estimates shown here.
  • Caveats: costs overlap, so the waterfall is an attribution, not a sum of independent savings. About 2 s of the gap stayed unattributed after the reviewers' trims. The 1-GPU Q13 baseline was not fully warm, which flatters 4-GPU scaling slightly.
