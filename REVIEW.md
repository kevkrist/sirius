## Findings

### R3 - P2, closed: confirmation attribution honors the B/B drift control

Previously, [s5_compare.py](tools/experiments/s5_compare.py), lines 585-605, emitted the confirmation's `verdict`, `within_3pct` and `gain_at_least_5pct` independently of its same-query B/B result. `baseline_drift_within_3pct` was computed only on the separate control row and never consumed. The design, line 108, requires B/B point ratio in [0.97, 1.03] and an interval including one: "otherwise attribution is inconclusive."

Concrete trigger represented in the CPU test source: five B/B ratios of 1.01 have interval [1.01, 1.01], so the noise control fails even though the point lies inside 3%. Before the fix, a confirmation with five ratios of 1.0 still reported `nonregression_observed`; five ratios of 0.94 additionally reported a qualifying gain. This was a reporting/attribution defect, not a defect in the ratio or Student-t calculation, and no measured campaign is claimed to exhibit it.

Required property: an acceptance/attribution verdict cannot pass when its required B/B guard fails. Independently verified the minimal project-runner change at lines 606-616: it copies the same query's control result onto the confirmation; on failure it emits `inconclusive`, null acceptance flags and `same_query_baseline_control_failed`, preserving the ratios, intervals and block observations. The existing CPU test now checks apparent nonregression, gain and regression, unchanged raw estimates/control row/diagnostics, and independence of the other query. The developer's recorded run reports 18 CPU tests passed in 1.219 seconds; I inspected the code/assertions and matching hashes without executing tests. No GPU validation is needed for this summary-only remedy. R3 is closed for the project runner; the immutable external runner remains preserved for its completed correctness-only campaign, which did not use confirmation attribution.
